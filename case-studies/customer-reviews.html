PORTFOLIO PROJECT · VOICE OF CUSTOMER

Customer Review Intelligence

Turning product feedback into inspectable customer evidence, with a clear boundary between analysis and experimentation.

A product-level review exploration prototype brings feedback, product attributes and sentiment together. Its value is easier access to evidence; its limits are a small sample and an evaluation process still to be built.

The customer problem

Product reviews hold useful detail about customer experience, but reading individual comments makes it hard to compare products consistently. This exploratory work organises review text and product attributes so a reader can inspect sentiment, feedback and product-level questions.

  • What feedback is available for a particular product?
  • Which reviews warrant closer investigation for complaints or satisfaction signals?
  • How can product attributes and review text be inspected together?
  • What evidence is still needed before making a personalisation or messaging recommendation?

Data, scope & my contribution

The checked-in Sephora-derived files contain 245 review rows covering 49 product IDs, and a separate 50-product catalogue. Joining on product ID retains 245 rows. There are no duplicate complete review rows or missing review-text values in this file check. The original collection method and sampling frame are not documented.

The repository README quotes 252 reviews and 51 products; the files currently contain 245 and 50. This page uses the inspected files. A historical 8,000+ review analysis is not evidenced by this implementation and is not claimed here.

My showcased work is the Python preparation, sentiment exploration and product-query prototype in the repository. Individual versus team contribution is not documented. This is portfolio evidence, not a deployed customer service or a measured marketing intervention.

Why semantic representations? Customers can describe a similar issue using different words. Embeddings are a plausible way to retrieve related text beyond exact keywords. That is the rationale for the experimental component; retrieval quality and end-to-end answer grounding have not yet been demonstrated.

Preparation & analytical decisions

  • Load review and catalogue CSVs; join using product ID.
  • Lowercase and tokenise review text, remove non-alphabetic tokens and stopwords, and lemmatise.
  • Combine processed text with ingredients and highlights, then apply sentiment classification.
  • Create chunk representations and store embeddings; keep this separate from claims about how answers are retrieved.

The key trade-off is that aggressive cleaning can remove negation or context that a transformer sentiment model needs. The next iteration should compare classification on raw versus cleaned reviews using a manually labelled sample.

The sample is strongly weighted towards high ratings: 206 of 245 reviews are four or five stars (84.1%). That makes negative-experience review coverage especially important when assessing the prototype. It does not establish customer satisfaction for the wider product population.

The code supports counts, ingredients, product comparisons and complaint-summary requests. No validated recurring complaint ranking, repurchase prediction or measured conversion improvement is claimed.

Skin-type references in reviews are customer statements. The current suitability branch relies on keyword presence and can overstate its conclusion. It should be redesigned to quote relevant feedback and uncertainty; it is not evidence of clinical suitability.

Proposed business use

  • Use low-rated reviews as a manual investigation queue, then code recurring themes with counts and product context.
  • Give product and marketing teams traceable review excerpts rather than unsupported generated conclusions.
  • Compare products only when sample sizes and the review mix make the comparison meaningful.
  • Treat repurchase intent as a separate annotation or modelling task; ratings alone do not establish repeat purchase.

Challenges & limitations

  • Small, selected and positively skewed sample; results cannot be generalised to all customers.
  • Binary sentiment does not capture mixed or aspect-specific feedback, and confidence is not calibrated business certainty.
  • The current query engine is not connected to Chroma retrieval; no retrieval benchmark or answer-grounding score is available.
  • T5 summaries and keyword suitability checks can produce misleading conclusions; quoted source evidence and human review are needed.
  • Collection permissions, sampling and individual/team ownership should be clarified before expanding the work.

What I would improve next

  • Preserve original wording and review identifiers; evaluate sentiment with manual labels and aspect-level categories.
  • Connect retrieval to the query path and measure relevant-document retrieval and grounded-answer quality.
  • Add topic and complaint coding with explicit denominators and uncertainty.
  • Evaluate personalisation separately, with defined success criteria and no unsupported suitability claims.

What this demonstrates

A customer-insight question translated into a structured text-analysis workflow, with clear boundaries between what is implemented, what the data supports and what needs evaluation. The employer value is the ability to turn unstructured feedback into inspectable evidence.
