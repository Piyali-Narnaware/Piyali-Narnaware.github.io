Business context & my role

Torrent Gas needed customer and commercial information to support expansion planning, customer follow-up and regular performance reporting. In my role as Assistant Manager – Marketing in Pune, I worked with SAP S/4HANA, CRM information and Excel analysis during September 2021–May 2023.

The analytical focus sat within a marketing and commercial role. It is presented here under the official employment title, with the reported analytical responsibilities made explicit.

  • How could customer and occupancy information improve planning assumptions?
  • Which CRM and payment patterns should inform follow-up?
  • How could recurring reporting require less manual preparation?
  • How could commercial information support expansion discussions?

Data & disclosure boundary

The work used SAP customer records, occupancy and household-density information, acquisition and consumption trends, CRM, billing and payment activity. Excel reconciliation also supported the validation and migration of 1,100+ customer records into SAP S/4HANA. No customer-level company data, internal reports or employer-endorsed results are reproduced here.

This professional case study is grounded in my supplied CV and career evidence bank. The percentages are reported outcomes, not independently audited measurements. Baselines, measurement windows and exact calculation definitions are not available in the public material.

Commercial forecasting

Problem: Expansion planning needed a more useful view of potential customer acquisition and demand. Approach: I combined SAP S/4HANA customer data, residential occupancy, household density and historical acquisition and consumption trends in Excel to develop domestic PNG customer-acquisition forecasts.

Why this approach: Bringing customer context alongside system records makes planning assumptions more transparent and easier to discuss. The public evidence does not document a specific statistical algorithm, so this is described as commercial forecasting rather than a named predictive model.

Output and use: Forecast information supported expansion planning and approximately 2,700 new domestic customer acquisitions. An approximately 30% improvement in forecast accuracy was reported; the baseline error metric and comparison period need confirmation.

Customer retention & payment behaviour

Problem: Retention and overdue accounts required an understanding of customer and payment behaviour. Approach: I analysed CRM, consumption, billing histories, outstanding balances and payment patterns in SAP and Excel to identify retention opportunities and customers at greater risk of delayed payment.

Why this approach: Customer history provides context for a balance or payment event. It can guide investigation and prioritisation, without treating every late payment as the same customer problem.

Reported result: Analysis contributed to actions associated with approximately 15% improvement in retention and approximately 15% reduction in overdue accounts. These are contributed outcomes; the effect of analysis is not isolated from other team actions.

Reporting automation

Before: Recurring reporting involved manual preparation of SAP export information. Change: Excel, Power Query and Pivot reporting were used to standardise preparation and monthly KPI reporting. After: The reported manual effort was approximately 40% lower.

Why Power Query: Repeatable transformations can replace repeated spreadsheet preparation and keep recurring reports consistent. The exact query sequence and time-study baseline are not published, so no invented hours-saved figure is used.

The output improved management visibility of acquisition, collections, retention and commercial performance. A fully evidenced process comparison would include preparation time, refresh failures and reconciliation checks as well as the percentage reduction.

Commercial expansion context

I evaluated CNG station opportunities using market demand, vehicle movement, customer potential, local competition, site observations and Excel-based commercial analysis. This supported feasibility decisions across 24 finalised stations and contributed to a reported 13% increase in CNG net sales.

The career evidence bank also records support for 30 industrial and 16 commercial customer conversions, plus coordination across Sales, Finance and Operations within a wider team of 25+ colleagues. These are reported programme-level outcomes and working context, not claims of sole ownership or experimental causality.

Challenges & limitations

  • Company data and internal outputs are unavailable for public demonstration.
  • Published material does not establish experimental causality or separate individual contribution from wider team action.
  • Metric definitions and measurement periods must be recoverable before using these figures as precise comparative claims.
  • The process diagram is a reconstruction of described work, not a screenshot of a production system.

What I would improve next

  • Create a consistent KPI dictionary with baselines, denominators and reporting periods.
  • Retain an auditable forecast-versus-actual comparison and a reporting time log.
  • Track follow-up cohorts and interventions so retention associations can be evaluated more clearly.
  • Document refresh checks and exception handling alongside automation steps.

What this demonstrates

Experience working with customer and commercial information inside an operating energy business. The strongest evidence is the connection between system data, planning, customer action and recurring reporting. SAP migration and cross-functional coordination add evidence of data quality and stakeholder communication.
